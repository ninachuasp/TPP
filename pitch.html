🧭 Detour — investor memo

Group trips are planned in group chats.
Group chats are where trips go to die.

Detour is a link-first, real-time planner for group road trips: everyone drops their pins, reels, and must-eats into one shared map and itinerary — and the group actually decides. No app store, no accounts. The link is the trip.

1

The problem

A four-person road trip today is planned across five disconnected surfaces: a WhatsApp thread, forty browser tabs, Google Maps saved-pin lists that can't be co-edited or exported, Instagram saves nobody re-opens, and one martyr's spreadsheet. Inspiration lives in one place, geography in another, and the group's decisions in none — so the loudest voice plans the trip, and half the saved gems never make it into a day.

Trip planners exist. They fail groups for a predictable reason: they demand that every traveler download an app and create an account before contributing a single pin. For a one-off trip with your in-laws, that's a wall.

2

The product

Everything below is built and running — prototyped on a real trip: a two-crew, ten-day Hokkaidō road trip where two travelers start, two join mid-way, and two stay on. That messy, real-world shape is exactly what nothing else handles.

liveLink-first collaborationOne URL, zero accounts. Real-time sync — a pin added in one browser appears on everyone's map in under a second.
livePaste anythingGoogle Maps URLs parse into placed pins; Instagram, TikTok, Tabelog, and booking links attach to stops as tappable chips.
liveRoute intelligencePer-day drive-time budgets that turn amber when a day is overstuffed. Numbered pins, per-day route lines.
liveCrew-aware daysDays know who's on the road — split arrivals and departures are a first-class concept, not a footnote.
liveVoting🔥 votes settle wishlist debates democratically instead of by whoever types fastest.
liveTrip-day modeCheck off visited stops — pins turn gold, and by the last day the map is a keepsake of where you actually went.
3

Why now

Travel inspiration has moved wholesale to short-form video — trips are now assembled from reels and TikToks — but planning tools still assume inspiration arrives as a search query. The gap between "saved it" and "we're going" is unowned. Whoever owns the surface where a group converts saved content into a committed itinerary owns the trip's booking intent — hotels, cars, and experiences — at the moment of highest signal.

4

Wedge, then expansion

Road trips are the wedge: they're the most collaborative trip type (shared car, shared days, contested stops), the most geographically structured (routes, drive times), and the worst served. The same primitives — shared map, crew timeline, voting — generalize to every multi-person trip: bachelorette weekends, family reunions, festival crews.

The compounding asset is the trip graph: which stops real groups chose together, in what order, at what time of year — consented, aggregated demand data that neither Google (single-player saves) nor Instagram (no geography) assembles today.

5

Business model

  • Free forever for planning — collaboration features are the growth loop; every trip invites 2–8 new users by link.
  • Booking affiliate — stays, rental cars, and experiences booked from inside the itinerary, at the moment the group commits.
  • Trip Pro — one-time per-trip upgrade: offline packs, expense splitting, printed keepsake map of the finished trip.
6

Where we are, honestly

A working full-stack prototype: real-time backend (Supabase), zero-dependency web client, one live trip in the field this October. No registered users, no revenue, no growth numbers yet — this memo is a thesis and a demo, not a traction story.
